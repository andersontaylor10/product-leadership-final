# Product Strategy & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | Contractors trust every data point in Meridian because it comes straight from the job site, captured once, clean and complete. |
| **Where to play** | Mid-size and large general contractors ($5M+ projects), starting where field data enters the system: superintendents and foremen capturing photos, status and daily logs. We are leaving out small contractors and minimalist field apps, and we won’t try to win on simplicity alone. |
| **How to win** | Complexity where you want it, simplicity where you need it. Field crews get capture as easy as a text, and that clean data flows into the deep integrations, compliance and reporting that mobile-first competitors can’t match and that customers can grow into. |
| **Capabilities required** | Field capture as fast and easy as a text message, deliberately not feature-rich. Turning simple field inputs into structured data that flows correctly into every downstream process and integration. |
| **Management systems** | Field coverage: the share of active projects with regular mobile field entries, and how often each project gets them, trending toward 100%. Downstream quality: manual edits and workarounds on field-sourced data, trending down. Monthly business review of accounts lost and won, including whether the mobile experience is helping win larger accounts. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not build new analytics or new ERP integrations this year. More complexity in the office is only as good as the data feeding it, and today that data breaks at the job site. Fixing field capture makes every existing integration more valuable for every customer, from mid-size to the largest. Adding new ones would split our 10-person team before the data chain is fixed.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Meridian the most trusted source of project data, from the first field entry to the final report.
>
> - **KR1:** Active projects with weekly mobile field entries: 5% → 50%
> - **KR2:** Field-sourced records needing manual correction in the office: 15% → 7%
> - **KR3:** Annual churn among mid-size customers ($5–50M projects): 14% → 8%

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** The internal contradiction between my hard no and my Where to Play. I said no to pushing toward the top 10 while also saying large contractors are part of where we play and tracking large-account wins. What I meant was that we aren't pursuing those large accounts by building more integrations, instead we are building towards the quality of data that will serve as a value-add even to our largest accounts.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** I’ll change the hard no so it’s about what we build, not who we serve: no new analytics or ERP integrations, but large customers get the field solution too, because data integrity has to be built into the enterprise product or they’ll leave for one that has it. I’ll defend the field-first bet as a provisional bet. In the first quarter, we’ll interview churned mid-size accounts to test whether the field gap drove their decision. If fewer than half cite field adoption or data problems, we revisit Where to Play before scaling the rollout.
